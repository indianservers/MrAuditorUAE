India: explicit 206C(1H) positive expectation boundary

The Income Tax Department's Finance Act 2025, section 72(c), records the inserted
proviso making section 206C(1H) inapplicable from 1 April 2025. The official source
was read on 30 October 2026:
https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-72-94

The liability reconciliation now flags a positive expected amount when its
explicit collector/TCS reference is 206C(1H) (case and whitespace normalized,
with an optional literal TCS: prefix) and its declared transaction date or
reporting month is on/after the cutoff. The raw expected, withheld, reported and
deposited observations remain visible even when all amounts agree. A separate
statutory scope issue blocks approval. An exception disposition cannot override
the boundary. The API also checks retained older result rows before approval or
new draft generation, without rewriting those saved rows.

Historical transactions and reporting months are not rejected merely because
settlement or review is later. An explicit zero expectation remains distinct;
actual positive collections against it still produce an ordinary variance.
Collectee credit observations remain evidence comparisons and do not determine
entitlement or recovery. This narrow named-provision check does not map arbitrary
aliases, infer receipt-trigger facts, compute thresholds/rates, qualify every TCS
provision, activate an India rule for UAE, or qualify native return formats.

Collector liability results retain the boundary source and cutoff in their scope
metadata and complete exports. Other selected roles do not receive that collector
scope metadata. The independent assessment/document controls remain required.
